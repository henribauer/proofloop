## Pitfalls

1. **Closed vendors.** Wappalyzer's ToS forbids embedding its data in a customer-facing product. BuiltWith's ToS limits use to internal business purposes and forbids public distribution and AI use. Neither fits a public demo. (https://www.wappalyzer.com/terms/ ; https://builtwith.com/terms)
2. **GPL data.** The fingerprint data is GPL-3.0, and the MIT wappalyzergo wrapper inherits it from its upstreams. If Proofloop ships it, GPL terms may apply (my inference, not legal advice). A small own table with vendor-doc links avoids this. (https://github.com/enthec/webappanalyzer)
3. **Passive HTML misses runtime-only signals.** The Pipedrive entry matches only the global `LeadBooster`. wappalyzergo's `Fingerprint` reads headers and body only. You need a rendered browser to see it. (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/enthec/webappanalyzer/main/src/technologies/p.json ; https://github.com/projectdiscovery/wappalyzergo)
4. **Generic patterns give false positives.** The DB's own README warns that short patterns "can cause applications to be identified incorrectly" and uses `confidence` tags. Accuracy figures online are vendor or blog claims. TechnologyChecker's "86% vs 64%" is a competitor's marketing and I found no independent study. (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/enthec/webappanalyzer/main/README.md)
5. **Consent banners change what you see.** Compliant sites gate scripts (Cookiebot `type="text/plain"`), so you get a false negative for the loaded state. Many sites also track before consent: one peer-reviewed study found ">75% of tracking activities" before a choice or after "reject all" (not a count of sites). So "not detected" and "detected" both describe only the pre-consent view. Clicking "accept" is a judgment call; I recommend not clicking. (https://www.cookiebot.com/en/manual-implementation/ ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.08779)
6. **Law: access line.** § 202a StGB punishes access to data "besonders gesichert" by "Überwindung der Zugangssicherung". Public pages are outside it. Logins, paywalls and bot-challenge circumvention are not. (https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stgb/__202a.html)
7. **robots.txt is policy, not law.** RFC 9309 says the rules "are not a form of access authorization". A 4xx on robots.txt means the crawler "MAY access any resources"; a 5xx means "MUST assume complete disallow". The EDPB draft Guidelines 03/2026 (genAI scraping only, consultation to 20261030) say scraping despite clear website restrictions is harder to justify. I apply that by analogy (inference). Honour Disallow and label the page "not assessed". (https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9309.html ; https://www.edpb.europa.eu/public-consultations/guidelines-032026-on-web-scraping-in-the-context-of-generative-ai_en)
8. **Public does not mean free of GDPR.** Staff names and emails in HTML are still personal data. The EDPB press coverage says public availability alone does not justify reuse. Store only vendor hostnames and a redacted snippet. § 25 TDDDG covers the visitor's device and does not govern a crawler, but it is why compliant sites gate scripts (my inference). (https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ttdsg/__25.html)

Not retrieved: German case law on scraping (e.g. Paperboy, I ZR 259/00; only a database listing found), TDM litigation, and the DSK telemedia guidance. Treat the legal notes as orientation, not advice.

## Label rules

**Observed fact.** Allowed only when the page was fetched on a stated date and the snippet is stored, in one of three forms. (a) A script or iframe `src` host from the vendor docs, e.g. "the contact form on page X is a HubSpot form (js.hsforms.net, `hbspt.forms.create`, portalId N)". (b) A gated tag, "Cookiebot-gated script present, loads after consent". (c) A booking or chat widget host. Say "embedded from", not "uses as its CRM".

**Likely.** A DB match below 100% confidence, or a DNS TXT/MX record. This is optional, and I rate it passive and legal but outside "what the browser loads".

**Not observed.** The default for everything else. Never write "does not use".

**Never infer.** The internal CRM, ticketing or mailbox. Whether a form feeds a workflow. Whether anyone staffs the chat. Volumes, response times, workload, conversion, revenue, staffing. A HubSpot form does not prove HubSpot CRM workflows. A Calendly embed does not prove calendar sync.

**Never do.** Log in. Submit a form. Open the chat. Pick a booking slot. Opening the chat or picking a slot can create a lead in the company's real system.
